# Jobs To Be Done

People don't buy products, they hire them to make progress in a situation. Find
the situation and the struggle, and the roadmap stops being a request queue.

> "When I *[situation]*, I want to *[motivation]*, so I can *[expected outcome]*."

## When to use

- Requested features keep shipping without moving adoption
- You don't know what customers actually replaced when they switched to you
- Positioning feels wrong and the competitor set seems to be the wrong question

## When *not* to use

- When you need a market size figure; this produces qualitative insight
- With no access to customers who switched recently
- As a template exercise in a workshop — without interviews it is fiction

## Facilitation outline (interviews over 2–4 weeks)

1. Recruit people who switched *recently*. Recall decays fast and irrecoverably.
2. Interview the timeline backwards: the moment of purchase, then what happened
   before it, then the first thought that something had to change.
3. Look for the four forces: push of the situation, pull of the new solution,
   anxiety about switching, habit of the present.
4. Cluster interviews into jobs, not personas.
5. Write each job as the sentence above and test it against a fresh interview.

## Common pitfalls

- Asking what customers want instead of what they did
- Interviewing non-switchers, who reconstruct rather than recall
- Collapsing jobs into demographics, which reintroduces the problem
- Producing job statements so generic they justify any roadmap
