# Force Field Analysis

Lewin's observation: a stalled change is rarely short of reasons to proceed. It
is held by restraints nobody has named. Weakening a restraint moves the system;
adding pressure usually does not.

```
driving forces  →→→  │  ←←←  restraining forces
      (weights)      │      (weights)
```

## When to use

- A change has stalled despite obvious benefits and repeated encouragement
- Before a rollout, to find what will quietly hold it back
- When leadership's instinct is to push harder

## When *not* to use

- When the change is genuinely not worth doing — this assumes the goal is settled
- As a substitute for talking to the people being changed
- For finding the cause of a failure; that is [Five Whys](Five_Whys.md)

## Facilitation outline (90 min)

1. (10 min) State the change as a specific end state, not a direction.
2. (25 min) List driving forces, then restraining forces. Restraints take longer —
   push past the polite ones.
3. (20 min) Weight each force 1–5. Weight independently before discussing, or the
   loudest force becomes the heaviest.
4. (20 min) Sort by weight. Look only at the top restraints.
5. (15 min) For each, name an action that *removes* it. Adding drivers is the
   reflex and usually the wrong move.

## Common pitfalls

- Building tidy symmetric lists that hide one dominant restraint
- Weighting by volume of discussion rather than by actual force
- Responding with more communication when the restraint is structural
- Never revisiting: the balance shifts as the change proceeds
