# Six Thinking Hats

Everyone thinks in the same mode at the same time, in turn — instead of each
person defending a fixed role for the whole meeting.

| Hat | Mode |
|---|---|
| ⚪ White | facts and data; what do we know, what is missing |
| 🔴 Red | feelings and intuition, stated without justification |
| ⚫ Black | caution; what could fail, what does not fit |
| 🟡 Yellow | value and feasibility; why this could work |
| 🟢 Green | alternatives and new ideas |
| 🔵 Blue | process; managing the sequence and summarising |

## When to use

- A discussion has hardened into advocates versus critics
- The same person is always cast as the sceptic and dismissed accordingly
- An idea needs proper exploration before judgement

## When *not* to use

- Two-person conversations
- Groups that will reject the ceremony outright — the frame needs buy-in
- When you need a ranked decision; this produces a rich discussion, not an order

## Facilitation outline (90 min)

1. (5 min) Blue: state the question and the hat sequence.
2. (15 min) White: facts only. Park every opinion.
3. (5 min) Red: gut reactions, one round, no justification allowed.
4. (15 min) Yellow, then (15 min) Black. This order matters — critique after value.
5. (20 min) Green: alternatives, informed by what Black surfaced.
6. (15 min) Blue: summarise and hand off to a decision method.

## Common pitfalls

- Letting one person stay under Black while others move on
- Skipping Red, which is where unspoken resistance actually lives
- Running Black before Yellow, which kills ideas before they are understood
- Ending without step 6, so the discussion never becomes a decision
