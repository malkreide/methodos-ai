# Pre-Mortem Analysis

Before committing, declare the plan already dead and ask the team to write the
obituary. Framing failure as given, rather than hypothetical, is what makes
people say the thing they were not going to say.

## When to use

- Just before a launch, migration, reorganisation, or large investment
- When the plan looks agreed and nobody is raising objections — the danger sign
- Early enough that the answers can still change the plan

## When *not* to use

- After the decision is locked; you will generate resentment, not risk data
- Without psychological safety — you will collect only comfortable risks
- Repeatedly on the same plan with the same people; the effect wears off

## Facilitation outline (90 min)

1. (5 min) "It is twelve months from now. The project failed completely. This
   is not a hypothetical — it happened." Say it in the past tense and mean it.
2. (15 min) Silent writing: each participant lists the reasons it failed.
   Individually and in silence, before anyone speaks.
3. (30 min) Round-robin, one reason each, until the room is empty of reasons.
4. (20 min) Cluster and rank by plausibility times damage.
5. (20 min) For the top items: a mitigation, an owner, and the earliest signal
   that would tell you it is happening.

## Common pitfalls

- Letting the sponsor speak first, which anchors everyone else
- Skipping the silent-writing step, which is where the non-obvious risks come from
- Producing a risk register nobody revisits — step 5's early-warning signal is
  what makes it operational
- Running it as a formality after the contract is signed
