# Feedforward

A short exchange about the future instead of the past. A person names one
behaviour of their own they want to improve, asks someone else for two
suggestions they could try from now on, listens without commenting on the
ideas, and says thank you. Then the roles swap. Nobody talks about what went
wrong before, so there is nothing to dispute and no reason to defend oneself.
In a group, each pair takes about two minutes and then everyone finds a new
partner, so in ten to fifteen minutes each participant collects several sets of
suggestions and gives as many.

The exercise comes from the executive coach Marshall Goldsmith, who describes
it in his article "Try Feedforward Instead of Feedback", published on his
website and widely reprinted, and in his 2007 book *What Got You Here Won't Get
You There* (with Mark Reiter). His argument is that the past cannot be changed
while the future can, that people take suggestions about the future less
personally than verdicts on their past, and that ideas can come from people who
do not know the person well, because the suggestions concern the task rather
than the person's history. He reports having run the exercise with tens of
thousands of leaders.

## When to use

- A person already knows which behaviour they want to change, for example
  delegating, listening, staying calm, or involving others in decisions, and
  wants practical ideas rather than another discussion of examples
- After a 360-degree review or an appraisal, so that a manager can turn a
  finding into ongoing, low-stakes requests for ideas from colleagues
- At the start of a leadership course, a staff day or a workshop, as a quick
  warm-up that gives everyone something useful for their own development
- In classes or teacher teams where criticism is met with excuses, to practise
  asking for and accepting suggestions without justifying oneself
- When a group's feedback habits have become a ritual of listing past
  failures and people have stopped listening

## When *not* to use

- **A specific incident has to be addressed**: a complaint, a missed handover,
  a moment that went wrong and needs naming. Describe the situation, the
  behaviour and its effect with [SBI Feedback](SBI_Feedback.md)
- **The team wants to review how it worked together** over a sprint, term or
  project. That is a team retrospective such as
  [Start, Stop, Continue](Start_Stop_Continue.md), not one person's habit
- **The person does not yet know what to work on**, or wants to learn how
  others see them. Start with a 360-degree review or a
  [Johari Window](Johari_Window.md) session, then use feedforward on what it
  reveals
- **Someone needs to work through a situation in depth** and find their own
  way forward. That calls for a coaching conversation, for example with the
  [GROW Model](GROW_Model.md)
- **The object of the feedback is a piece of work**, such as a draft, a plan or
  a lesson design. Use a [Tuning Protocol](Tuning_Protocol.md)
- **Serious performance or conduct problems.** These need a proper
  conversation with a manager and, where relevant, formal procedures

## Facilitation outline (about 20 minutes for a group)

Participants stand or sit so they can move freely. Have paper or a phone ready
for notes. Keep time centrally and announce each partner change.

1. (3 min) **Choose one behaviour.** Ask each person to decide, silently, on
   one behaviour they want to change that would make a real difference to
   their work or life, and to phrase it briefly: "I want to be better at
   letting others finish before I speak."
2. (1 min) **Explain the rules.** No talking about the past, even between
   people who know each other well. The person asking does not comment on the
   suggestions, not even with "good idea", and only says thank you. The person
   giving replies "you're welcome".
3. (2 min per pair) **Exchange.** Person A names the behaviour and asks for two
   suggestions for the future. B gives two, A notes them and says thank you.
   Then B names their own behaviour and A gives two suggestions.
4. (10–12 min) **Rotate.** Everyone finds a new partner and repeats step 3.
   Most people manage five to seven exchanges in this time.
5. (3–5 min) **Debrief.** Ask the whole group how it felt to receive and to
   give, and what was different from ordinary feedback. Invite each person to
   pick one or two ideas to try in the coming week.

As a one-to-one practice, a manager can use the same steps with individual
colleagues every few weeks: name the behaviour, ask for ideas, thank them,
and later tell them what they tried. Online, breakout rooms of two with a
timer replace the rotation.

## Common pitfalls

- Choosing a vague aim ("be a better leader") instead of one behaviour others
  could actually suggest something for
- Partners sliding into stories about past meetings, which brings back the
  arguing the exercise is meant to avoid
- The person asking reacting to each idea ("we tried that", "that won't work
  here") instead of just noting it and saying thank you
- Suggestions turning into criticism in disguise ("stop being so…")
- Treating the exercise as complete in itself: without trying an idea and
  checking back with colleagues later, nothing changes

## How it differs from its neighbours

- [SBI Feedback](SBI_Feedback.md) describes one past situation, what the
  person did and what effect it had. Feedforward deliberately leaves the past
  out and asks only for ideas for the future
- [Start, Stop, Continue](Start_Stop_Continue.md) is a team looking back on
  how it worked together and agreeing joint changes; feedforward is about one
  individual's own behaviour
- [GROW Model](GROW_Model.md) is a coaching conversation in which questions
  help a person find their own answer; in feedforward other people offer
  suggestions and there is no conversation about them
- [Tuning Protocol](Tuning_Protocol.md) has colleagues examine a piece of work
  against its goals for about an hour; feedforward takes two minutes and is
  about a behaviour, not a product
- [Johari Window](Johari_Window.md) helps a group learn how its members see
  one another; feedforward starts once the person already knows what they
  want to change
- [Troika Consulting](Troika_Consulting.md) gives a person ten minutes of
  discussion by two colleagues about a challenge they face; feedforward
  exchanges are much shorter, in pairs, and limited to two suggestions about
  one personal behaviour
- [Working Out Loud](Working_Out_Loud.md) is a group meeting weekly for
  months; feedforward is a single exercise or a lightweight habit
