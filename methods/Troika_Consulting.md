# Troika Consulting

Peer help in threes, with the roles rotating so each person gets a turn. In each
round one person is the *client*: they describe a challenge they are working on
and say what help they would like. The other two are *consultants*: they ask a
few questions to understand it, and then, with the client sitting with their
back to them, discuss the challenge between themselves and offer ideas and
advice. The client turns round at the end and says what was useful. Then the
roles move on, until all three have been the client.

Turning one's back is the trick in this method. Without eye contact the client
cannot nod, frown or interrupt, so they hear the ideas instead of answering
them, and the consultants talk about the problem more frankly than they would
to the person's face. The short time per round keeps the advice to practical
next steps.

Troika Consulting is one of the Liberating Structures collected by Henri
Lipmanowicz and Keith McCandless and described in their 2014 book *The
Surprising Power of Liberating Structures*. The English Liberating Structures
site gives about 30 to 35 minutes in all. The German-language site gives
slightly longer rounds and credits the improvisation teacher Keith Johnstone,
among others, as an inspiration.

## When to use

- Many people in a group each have a current challenge of their own, and the
  meeting or course cannot give every one of them individual attention
- At the start of a team meeting, a training day or a network meeting, to make
  the session useful to each participant's own work straight away
- On leadership programmes and courses, so that participants practise asking
  for help and giving it, not just listening to the trainer
- In a classroom or seminar where students working on separate projects get
  stuck at different points
- When people habitually answer advice with "yes, but…" and need a format in
  which they simply listen

## When *not* to use

- **One difficult case that needs real depth**, such as a pupil, a family or a
  staff situation with a long history. Ten minutes will not do it justice; use
  [Collegial Case Consultation](Collegial_Case_Consultation.md), which gives one
  case 45 minutes or more, with a moderator and a larger group
- **Sensitive or confidential matters** (personnel issues, child protection,
  anything with legal consequences). People cannot choose who lands in their
  trio, and these matters belong in the proper procedures
- **A shared question for the whole group.** Troika gives each person help with
  their own challenge. When the aim is to gather everyone's ideas on one common
  question, use [1-2-4-All](One_Two_Four_All.md)
- **Feedback on a finished or nearly finished piece of work.** Examining a draft
  against its goals takes the material, the time and the focus of a
  [Tuning Protocol](Tuning_Protocol.md)
- **When the help needed is a specialist's**, such as legal, medical or
  technical expertise that the people in the room do not have

## Facilitation outline (about 30 minutes, any number of trios)

Set out three chairs per group, without tables. Show the steps and timings
where everyone can see them, and keep time centrally for all trios.

1. (5 min) **Invitation.** Ask everyone to think, silently, about a challenge
   they are working on right now: what it is, what next step they are
   considering, and what help they would like. Then form trios, mixing people
   from different teams or roles where possible. Demonstrate turning the chair
   round and say that it is part of the method, not rude.
2. (1–2 min) **The client describes the challenge.** The first client explains
   the challenge and the help they want. The consultants listen.
3. (1–2 min) **Clarifying questions.** The consultants ask questions only to
   understand. No advice yet, and no suggestions dressed up as questions.
4. (4–5 min) **Consultation.** The client turns their chair so their back faces
   the consultants and stays silent, taking notes if they like. The two
   consultants talk to each other about the challenge: what they notice, what
   might be behind it, what the client could try. They speak about the client
   in the third person, as if they were not there.
5. (1–2 min) **What was useful.** The client turns back and says what struck
   them and what they will take from it. The consultants do not argue for
   ideas the client did not pick up.
6. (2 × 7–10 min) **Rounds two and three.** The roles rotate and steps 2 to 5
   repeat until each person has been the client once.
7. (2 min) **Whole group.** Invite two or three people to say what the format
   gave them or what they will do next. Do not ask for everyone's challenges to
   be reported.

Online, trios go into breakout rooms, and the client switches off camera and
microphone during the consultation. Variants on the Liberating Structures site
include a faster "speed" version with shorter steps, and one in which the
consultants may only ask questions, never give advice.

## Common pitfalls

- Clients bringing a challenge that is someone else's, or a vague complaint,
  instead of something they can act on themselves
- Clarifying questions turning into advice ("Have you tried…?") before the
  client has turned round
- The client not turning round, or interrupting the consultants to correct
  them, which turns the round back into an ordinary conversation
- Consultants talking to the client's back rather than to each other
- Letting the first round overrun, so the third person gets no proper turn
- Trios of people who work side by side every day, who share the same blind
  spots

## How it differs from its neighbours

- [Collegial Case Consultation](Collegial_Case_Consultation.md) — one person's
  case gets 45 to 60 minutes with a moderator, a case owner and several
  consultants in fixed phases. Troika is three people, ten minutes each, no
  moderator, and everyone gets a turn
- [1-2-4-All](One_Two_Four_All.md) — another Liberating Structure, which
  gathers input from everyone on one shared question; Troika gives each person
  help with a different question of their own
- [Peer Assist](Peer_Assist.md) — a team about to start something invites
  people who have done it before, for half a day or more; Troika is a quick
  exchange among whoever is in the room
- [Working Out Loud](Working_Out_Loud.md) — a small group meets weekly over
  months to support each member's personal goal and network; Troika is a single
  half-hour session
- [Tuning Protocol](Tuning_Protocol.md) — colleagues examine a concrete piece of
  work against its goals while the author listens; Troika is about a challenge
  a person faces, not about reviewing a document or product
