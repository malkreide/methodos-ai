# Eisenhower Matrix

Two axes, four rules. The insight is not the grid — it is noticing how little
of your week reaches the important-but-not-urgent quadrant.

|  | **Urgent** | **Not urgent** |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | Do now | **Schedule** — the quadrant that decays quietly |
| **Not important** | Delegate | Delete |

## When to use

- Your day is consumed by interruptions and the durable work never starts
- A lead needs to see which of their tasks genuinely require them
- Weekly planning, as a fast filter before committing the calendar

## When *not* to use

- Comparing large pieces of product work — use [RICE](RICE_Scoring.md) or [MoSCoW](MoSCoW_Method.md)
- When everything genuinely is urgent; that is a capacity problem, not a sorting one
- As a team ritual — it is a personal instrument and works best that way

## Facilitation outline (30 min, solo)

1. (10 min) Dump every open task. Unfiltered.
2. (10 min) Place each one. Ask "urgent to whom?" — borrowed urgency is the main distortion.
3. (5 min) Count how many hours this week land in Schedule. That number is the point.
4. (5 min) Book those hours in the calendar now, or they will not happen.

## Common pitfalls

- Marking everything important, which collapses the grid into a to-do list
- Confusing someone else's urgency with your importance
- Never using Delete, so the fourth quadrant becomes a graveyard you re-sort weekly
- Doing the sort but not step 4
