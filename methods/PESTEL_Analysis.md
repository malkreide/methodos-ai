# PESTEL Analysis

Six external forces you do not control. The point is to find the ones that
would never come up in a discussion about competitors.

| | |
|---|---|
| **P**olitical | stability, policy direction, trade posture |
| **E**conomic | growth, rates, inflation, currency, labour cost |
| **S**ocial | demographics, values, working patterns |
| **T**echnological | substitution, automation, platform shifts |
| **E**nvironmental | climate exposure, resource cost, disclosure duties |
| **L**egal | regulation, liability, employment and data law |

## When to use

- Entering a new country or heavily regulated market
- Multi-year investment horizons, where macro forces dominate industry ones
- Periodic strategy review, to catch drift in the operating environment

## When *not* to use

- Short-horizon or tactical decisions — the macro picture will not have moved
- Industry-structure questions; that is [Porter's Five Forces](Porters_Five_Forces.md)
- When nobody will own the follow-up; an unowned PESTEL is a news summary

## Facilitation outline (half a day plus desk research)

1. (Before) Assign one force per person for desk research. Sources, not opinions.
2. (45 min) Present per force. No debate yet, just findings.
3. (45 min) For each item, rate likelihood and impact over the chosen horizon.
4. (30 min) Keep only the high-high items. Everything else goes into an appendix.
5. (30 min) Convert survivors into either a monitored indicator or a planning assumption.

## Common pitfalls

- Listing headlines instead of analysing what they change for *this* organisation
- Skipping the horizon definition, so items of different timescales get mixed
- Producing a document nobody revisits after the strategy offsite
- Letting Legal and Environmental be thin because nobody in the room owns them
