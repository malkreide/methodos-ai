# Business Model Canvas

Nine blocks on one page. The value is not in filling them in — it is in seeing
which blocks are load-bearing assumptions rather than facts.

| Right side (value delivery) | Left side (value creation) |
|---|---|
| Customer Segments · Value Propositions · Channels · Customer Relationships · Revenue Streams | Key Resources · Key Activities · Key Partners · Cost Structure |

## When to use

- A new product, startup, or internal venture whose commercial logic is implicit
- Comparing two or three candidate models side by side before committing
- Onboarding a cross-functional team onto how the business actually earns money

## When *not* to use

- As evidence that customers want it — the canvas describes, it does not validate
- For competitive positioning; use [Porter's Five Forces](Porters_Five_Forces.md)
- For a mature, well-understood business where nothing on the page is in question

## Facilitation outline (3 hours)

1. (20 min) Customer Segments and Value Propositions first. Everything else follows from these two.
2. (60 min) Fill the remaining seven blocks. Keep sticky notes movable.
3. (30 min) Mark every note that is an *assumption* rather than a known fact.
   There are almost always more of them than the room expects.
4. (40 min) For the three riskiest assumptions, name the cheapest test that would kill them.
5. (30 min) Sketch one alternative model that makes a different bet and compare.

## Common pitfalls

- Treating a completed canvas as a validated plan
- One canvas covering several customer segments with genuinely different economics
- Skipping step 3, which is where the actual thinking happens
- Cost Structure written as a wish rather than derived from Key Resources and Activities
