# After Action Review

Four questions about one event, asked while people still remember it accurately.

1. What did we **expect** to happen?
2. What **actually** happened?
3. **Why** did those differ?
4. What will we **do differently** next time?

Question 3 is the review. Questions 1 and 2 exist to make it answerable.

## When to use

- Right after a launch, incident response, exercise, or customer escalation
- When one discrete episode holds lessons worth carrying to the next one
- Across ranks, when the facilitator can credibly suspend hierarchy for an hour

## When *not* to use

- For slow-building patterns across many iterations — use [Start, Stop, Continue](Start_Stop_Continue.md)
- More than a few days late; recall degrades and reconstruction takes over
- While the incident is still running

## Facilitation outline (60 min)

1. (5 min) Ground rules: no rank in the room, no blame, and it is on the record.
2. (10 min) Reconstruct what was expected — from what was written *before*, not from memory.
3. (15 min) Establish what happened, on a timeline, from evidence.
4. (20 min) Work the gaps between the two. Each gap is a candidate lesson.
5. (10 min) Convert lessons into actions with named owners, or they are not lessons.

## Common pitfalls

- Skipping step 2, which makes step 4 impossible and turns the review into storytelling
- Letting the most senior person speak first
- Confusing "what went wrong" with "who was wrong"
- No owner on the actions, so the next event repeats the episode
